Opening the Black Box

How researchers try to explain the decisions of GPT, Claude, Gemini, and LLaMA, why it is so hard, and how validation and metrics build trust while explanations are still catching up.
REALITY CHECK
25%

When Claude 3.7 Sonnet used a hint to answer, its written reasoning admitted it only 25% of the time. DeepSeek R1 did so 39% of the time (Anthropic, 2025b).

1

WHAT IS EXPLAINABLE AI AND WHY IT MATTERS

Explainable AI (XAI)

Methods that let people understand why a model produced a given output, either by tracing what happens inside the model or by explaining its behavior from the outside.

Two levels. Interpretability looks inside the model. Explainability gives a reason a person can act on.

Trust

People rely on a model's answer only when they can check how it got there. Hundreds of millions now use chatbots daily.

Accountability

US lenders must give specific reasons for denying credit. The EU AI Act adds transparency duties for AI systems.

Safety

Looking inside a model can catch hidden problems, such as reward hacking or deception, that its final answer conceals.

Bias and debugging

Explanations show which inputs drove a decision, so teams can find unfair patterns and fix the cause, not just the symptom.

2

CHALLENGES IN EXPLAINABILITY

Why LLM decisions are hard to explain

Opacity and scale

Knowledge is spread across billions of weights, and each neuron mixes many unrelated concepts. No single part explains an output.

Unfaithful explanations

25–39%

How often reasoning models admitted a hint they used. Written reasoning is not proof of real reasoning.

Data and bias

Models absorb the biases in their training data. A model can sound neutral when asked directly but still treat groups differently in its decisions.

Monitoring can erode

OpenAI reports that GPT-6 Astra's chain-of-thought monitorability decreased compared with GPT-5.6 Sol, so reading its reasoning reveals less.

Regulatory pressure

The EU AI Act phases in duties faster than explanation methods mature.

Aug 2026Article 50 transparency rules
Dec 2027High-risk system rules, delayed by the Digital Omnibus

VALIDATION AND PERFORMANCE METRICS

Trust comes from evidence, not from explanations alone
STEP 1 · VALIDATE

Test on unseen data

Held-out test sets and cross-validation show how a model performs on data it never learned from.

STEP 2 · MEASURE

Pick metrics that fit the risk

AccuracyPrecisionRecallF1 scoreROC-AUCCalibration
STEP 3 · STRESS-TEST

Attack and audit

Red teaming, jailbreak regression tests, and bias audits check behavior beyond the benchmark.

STEP 4 · DOCUMENT

Earn trust

System cards and model cards publish the results, the known limits, and what was not tested.

How metrics support explanations

Explanations say why. Metrics say how often the model is right, for whom, and at what cost.

Explanations need validation too. A feature or reasoning trace counts only if tests show it truly drives the output.

Neither is enough alone. High accuracy can hide unfair errors, and a fluent explanation can hide the real cause.

CURRENT SOLUTIONS FROM INDUSTRY LEADERS

From reading outputs to looking inside the model

Anthropic

Claude
  • Feature maps. Sparse autoencoders found millions of human-readable concepts inside Claude 3 Sonnet (2024).
  • Circuit tracing. Attribution graphs showed Claude 3.5 Haiku plans rhymes before writing them (2025).
  • Honesty tests. Measured how faithfully reasoning models report their own reasoning.
Circuit-tracing tools released as open source.

OpenAI

GPT
  • Feature maps at scale. Trained a sparse autoencoder with 16 million features on GPT-4 (2024).
  • Chain-of-thought monitoring. Monitors read full reasoning traces to catch misbehavior.
  • Public disclosure. Astra's system card reports the drop in monitorability openly.
Explainability treated as a safety control, not just a feature.

Google DeepMind

Gemini and Gemma
  • Gemma Scope 2. Autoencoders and transcoders for every layer of Gemma 3, from 270M to 27B parameters (2025).
  • Largest open release. Needed about 110 petabytes of data and over 1 trillion trained parameters.
  • Use cases. Diagnosing jailbreaks, refusals, and hallucinations.
Open tools let outside researchers audit the model.

Meta

LLaMA
  • Open weights. Anyone can inspect the internals of LLaMA directly.
  • Community tools. Llama Scope released 256 sparse autoencoders for Llama-3.1-8B (Fudan University, 2024).
  • Independent checks. Researchers outside Meta can reproduce and verify findings on the same weights.
Openness turns explainability into a shared research effort.
The broader XAI toolkit

Feature attribution

SHAP and LIME score how much each input pushed a prediction up or down.

Best for smaller models

Attention maps

Show which words the model weighed most when producing an answer.

Attention is not the full explanation

Probing

Simple classifiers test whether a concept is stored inside a layer.

Finds a concept, not its use

Chain-of-thought

The model writes its reasoning step by step before answering.

Often unfaithful, 25–39%

KEY TAKEAWAYS

Explanations can mislead

A model's own account of its reasoning is not proof. It has to be checked against what the model actually does.

Metrics carry the weight today

Until inside-the-model tools mature, validation, the right metrics, and stress tests are the main evidence for trust.

Openness speeds progress

Open tools such as circuit tracing, Gemma Scope, and Llama Scope let independent researchers verify claims.
